# Seeding — lettuce (iceberg)

Status: **✅ germinated by 2026-08-07** (sown early August). First crop in the
project to sprout — the strawberries followed on 2026-08-07 as well.

*Correction: this was first recorded as germinating 2026-08-03. That was the
sow date, not the germination date; the observation is 08-07.*

## What just happened, and what it does and does not prove

The seed germinated at ~28 °C ambient, beating the thermodormancy warning in
[README.md](README.md). That warning was not wrong in general — lettuce
genuinely does stall above ~27 °C — but tolerance varies a lot by variety and
seed lot, and this lot clearly did not care. Worth remembering the next time
the guidance says "hold until it cools": try a couple of sponges anyway, the
seed is cheap.

**What it proves:** the sponge prep, the 2–3 mm tray water, the humidity and
the general setup all work. Seed put into these sponges germinates.

**What it does not prove:** it was, at the time, no evidence about the
strawberries — a different technique on a 14–30 day clock. *That question has
since been answered directly: the strawberries germinated on day 4 too. The
surface-sowing technique works.*

## Care from sprouting onward — the first 48 hours matter

Germination care and seedling care are opposites, and lettuce punishes the
delay:

1. **Light, immediately and plenty of it.** A sprouted lettuce in dim light
   stretches within a day or two, and a leggy seedling never fully recovers —
   it stays weak-stemmed and flops. Brightest indirect spot available; this
   is the strongest argument yet for getting the grow light in early.
2. **Start venting the dome now, and remove it within a few days.** Its job
   ended at germination. Kept sealed over sprouted seedlings it drives
   stretching and damping-off.
3. **Cooler is better now.** 28 °C makes seedlings stretch even in good light.
   The coolest spot in the house serves the lettuce and the strawberry tray
   equally well.
4. **Keep 2–3 mm of plain demin water** until the first *true* leaves — the
   two rounded cotyledons showing now are not true leaves.
5. **At first true leaves:** quarter-strength A+B, **EC 0.4–0.8, pH ~5.8**.
6. **Thin to the strongest seedling per sponge** at ~2 true leaves. Snip the
   losers at the base with scissors; pulling drags the keeper's roots.

## Transplant — sooner than the strawberries

**Gate:** roots through the sponge + 2 true leaves, typically **2–3 weeks
after sprouting**, so **≈21–28 August**.

Lettuce is still the **first crop into the tower**, though the margin shrank
when the strawberries germinated early too — they now follow at ≈31 Aug –
14 Sept rather than late September. The consequences are on the project, not
the plant:

- **The reservoir must be dosed by ~20 August**, not September, and the
  strawberries want it barely a week later.
- **⚠ pH-Down and EC calibration fluid are needed by ~14 August** to leave any
  slack. This is the single most time-critical item in the project. Without
  pH-Down there is no way to bring a freshly dosed reservoir to 6.0, and
  nothing can be transplanted.
- Dose to **EC 0.8–1.0, pH ~6.0** for these first transplants. With only
  lettuce in the tower, staying at the low end suits it (lettuce likes
  0.8–1.2); step up later as the peppers and strawberries arrive.
- It is a genuinely useful rehearsal: 2–3 plants on 7.5 L is a forgiving load
  for shaking out drip coverage, pod fit and sensor baselines before thirty
  plants depend on it.

## Iceberg specifics

- **Heading lettuce, not cut-and-come-again.** One harvest per pod, then the
  pod is empty — unlike basil or parsley. This is why succession matters:
  re-sow 2 sponges every 2–3 weeks to keep the 4 lettuce pods rotating.
- **~30–45 days pod-to-plate** once transplanted.
- **Heads are bulky** (~25 cm) for a 25×25×86 cm tower; expect neighbours to
  be crowded. Loose-leaf or butterhead would suit the tower better if the
  variety is ever reconsidered.
- **Tip burn** is the failure mode at the shared EC 1.4–1.6. Harvest heads
  young, keep airflow over the tower, and do not let the reservoir drift high
  between top-ups.
- **Bolting**: heat makes it run to seed instead of hearting. Autumn indoors
  is actually good timing for it; a hot spot next to a window is not.
