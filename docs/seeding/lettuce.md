# Seeding — lettuce (iceberg)

Status: **❌ DIED — reported 2026-08-31.** Sown 2026-08-03, germinated
2026-08-07, dead some time in the four weeks since. Re-sow needed — see the
post-mortem at the bottom of this doc, and **fix the cause before re-sowing**.

*Correction: this was first recorded as germinating 2026-08-03. That was the
sow date, not the germination date; the observation is 08-07.*

## What just happened, and what it does and does not prove

The seed germinated at ~28 °C ambient, beating the thermodormancy warning in
[README.md](README.md). That warning was not wrong in general — lettuce
genuinely does stall above ~27 °C — but tolerance varies a lot by variety and
seed lot, and this lot clearly did not care. Worth remembering the next time
the guidance says "hold until it cools": try a couple of sponges anyway, the
seed is cheap.

**What it proves:** the sponge prep, the 2–3 mm tray water, the humidity and
the general setup all work. Seed put into these sponges germinates.

**What it does not prove:** it was, at the time, no evidence about the
strawberries — a different technique on a 14–30 day clock. *That question has
since been answered directly: the strawberries germinated on day 4 too. The
surface-sowing technique works.*

## Care from sprouting onward — the first 48 hours matter

Germination care and seedling care are opposites, and lettuce punishes the
delay:

1. **Light, immediately and plenty of it.** A sprouted lettuce in dim light
   stretches within a day or two, and a leggy seedling never fully recovers —
   it stays weak-stemmed and flops. Brightest indirect spot available; this
   is the strongest argument yet for getting the grow light in early.
2. **Start venting the dome now, and remove it within a few days.** Its job
   ended at germination. Kept sealed over sprouted seedlings it drives
   stretching and damping-off.
3. **Cooler is better now.** 28 °C makes seedlings stretch even in good light.
   The coolest spot in the house serves the lettuce and the strawberry tray
   equally well.
4. **Keep 2–3 mm of plain demin water** until the first *true* leaves — the
   two rounded cotyledons showing now are not true leaves.
5. **At first true leaves:** quarter-strength A+B, **EC 0.4–0.8, pH ~5.8**.
6. **Thin to the strongest seedling per sponge** at ~2 true leaves. Snip the
   losers at the base with scissors; pulling drags the keeper's roots.

## Transplant — now voided by the loss

*The ≈21–28 August window below assumed the first sowing survived. It did not,
so lettuce no longer leads: **the strawberries are now the first crop into the
tower** at ≈31 Aug – 14 Sept, and re-sown lettuce transplants ~3 weeks after
whenever it goes back in.*

**Gate:** roots through the sponge + 2 true leaves, typically **2–3 weeks
after sprouting**.

- **The reservoir must be dosed before the strawberries are ready**, i.e. by
  **~25 August**. The deadline is strawberry-driven now rather than
  lettuce-driven, which buys about a week — not a reprieve.
- **⚠ pH-Down and EC calibration fluid are still the critical path.** Without
  pH-Down there is no way to bring a freshly dosed reservoir to 6.0, and
  nothing can be transplanted. Buy now; the date only moved by days.
- Dose to **EC 0.8–1.0, pH ~6.0** for these first transplants. With only
  lettuce in the tower, staying at the low end suits it (lettuce likes
  0.8–1.2); step up later as the peppers and strawberries arrive.
- It is a genuinely useful rehearsal: 2–3 plants on 7.5 L is a forgiving load
  for shaking out drip coverage, pod fit and sensor baselines before thirty
  plants depend on it.

## Iceberg specifics

- **Heading lettuce, not cut-and-come-again.** One harvest per pod, then the
  pod is empty — unlike basil or parsley. This is why succession matters:
  re-sow 2 sponges every 2–3 weeks to keep the 4 lettuce pods rotating.
- **~30–45 days pod-to-plate** once transplanted.
- **Heads are bulky** (~25 cm) for a 25×25×86 cm tower; expect neighbours to
  be crowded. Loose-leaf or butterhead would suit the tower better if the
  variety is ever reconsidered.
- **Tip burn** is the failure mode at the shared EC 1.4–1.6. Harvest heads
  young, keep airflow over the tower, and do not let the reservoir drift high
  between top-ups.
- **Bolting**: heat makes it run to seed instead of hearting. Autumn indoors
  is actually good timing for it; a hot spot next to a window is not.

## ⚠ Post-mortem — the lettuce died (reported 2026-08-31)

The lettuce germinated on 08-07 and was dead by the time it was reported on
08-31. The exact date of death is unrecorded, which itself is the first
lesson: **nothing was watching the tray.** It matters far beyond the lettuce
— the strawberries are in the same tray, in the same conditions.

*Note: the strawberries have survived the same four weeks, which shifts the
diagnosis. A cause that killed everything (cooked tray, total dry-out) is
unlikely; a cause that picks off the more delicate seedling — damping-off, or
lettuce-specific heat stress and bolting-out at 28 °C — fits better.*

### Diagnose before re-sowing — the symptom names the cause

| What you see | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Stem pinched, brown/black and thread-thin **right at the sponge surface**; seedling topples while its leaves still look green | **Damping-off** (fungal). By far the most likely culprit in a hot, humid, domed tray — and exactly the risk flagged when they sprouted | Remove dead material now. Dome off or fully vented. Airflow — a fan on low, aimed near them, not at them. Let the sponge surface dry slightly between waterings. Fresh clean tray water. |
| Whole seedling shrivelled, dry, crispy | **Dried out** — sponge tops went dry, or the dome came off too abruptly | 2–3 mm water maintained; never let sponge tops dry; vent gradually |
| Everything collapsed at once; tray warm to the touch | **Cooked** — 28 °C ambient plus sun on a domed tray passes 35 °C | Out of all direct sun, coolest spot in the house |
| Long, thin, pale stems that flopped over, base still white and intact | **Stretched (etiolated)** in weak light, then collapsed under its own weight | Brightest light available, immediately. This is the grow-light argument in its most literal form. |

### Act on the strawberries now, whatever the cause

They shared the tray, so assume they are exposed:

1. **Ventilate hard** — dome cracked wide or off. Humidity is what damping-off
   needs.
2. **Remove every dead seedling and any mould or algae** from the tray. Dead
   tissue is the fungal reservoir.
3. **Fresh tray water**, 2–3 mm, and do not let it creep higher.
4. **Move to the coolest, brightest spot available.** Both conditions fight
   the two most likely causes at once.
5. **Watch the stem bases daily**, not the leaves. Damping-off shows at the
   base first and kills within a day once it starts.

### Re-sowing lettuce

Cheap and fast, so re-sow 2–3 sponges — but **fix the cause first**, and use a
separate tray with clean water rather than the one that just lost a crop.
Transplant then lands ~3 weeks after the re-sow.

### The PoC did its job, in the worst possible way

This is precisely what the 5-sponge proof of concept existed to discover: that
the tray environment can kill seedlings. It surfaced that fact — but with 60
strawberry seeds already committed instead of 15, because the full sowing went
in ahead of the PoC. The lesson is not "the PoC was wrong"; it is that the
early-warning crop earned its place.
